Insurance agency SEO: how to get found on Google and generate more leads

A practical, step-by-step playbook for agency owners and marketing leads. Use it to audit your own agency, or hand it to whoever runs your website.

1. Why SEO matters for insurance agencies

Shared leads are sold to several agencies at once, and the cost per click on insurance keywords keeps rising. SEO gives you something different: a flow of exclusive, high-intent prospects who found your agency on their own.

For local agencies the best opportunity isn't national terms like "car insurance", where carriers spend heavily. It's the thousands of local, specific searches like "insurance agency in [town]", "restaurant insurance [city]" or "who insures older homes near me". Google favors businesses that are nearby, relevant and trusted on those searches, and that's where an established agency can win.

2. Win the map pack with Google Business Profile

For "near me" searches, the local 3-pack (the map with three businesses under it) often gets the click before the regular organic results do. Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the biggest factor in whether you show up there.

GBP optimization checklist

  • Primary category: "Insurance agency" is usually right. Add secondary categories such as "Auto insurance agency", "Home insurance agency" or "Life insurance agency" only if they match what you sell.
  • One verified profile per staffed office. Don't create profiles for virtual offices or for individual producers in the same office without understanding Google's guidelines.
  • Services and products: list every line you write, each with a short description.
  • Consistent name, address and phone (NAP) across your website, GBP and major directories.
  • Real photos of your team, office and community events, updated regularly.
  • Posts every week or two: seasonal coverage tips, community news, new carrier partners.
  • Q&A: add and answer the common questions yourself.
  • A location-specific landing page URL for each profile, not just your homepage.

To find these terms, start with Google Search Console (what you already rank for), Google's "People also ask" boxes, autocomplete, and your Google Ads search terms report if you run ads. Your producers are another great source: ask them which questions they answer every week.

4. Build location and line-of-business pages

Many agency websites have a single page that lists every line they write. That's hard to rank. Instead, build:

  • A page for each office: address, map, hours, team photos, local reviews, the towns you serve and the lines that office specializes in.
  • A page for each core line: auto, home, renters, umbrella, business owners (BOP), general liability, workers' comp, commercial auto, life.
  • Industry pages for commercial niches you're strong in, like contractors, restaurants, trucking or real estate.

Avoid doorway pages. Don't publish dozens of near-identical "car insurance in [town]" pages with only the town name changed. Google treats these as spam. Each page needs genuinely local, useful content: local risks, community involvement, staff, reviews and specific coverage advice.

Every page should have a clear title tag (e.g. "Business Insurance in Tampa, FL | [Agency]"), one H1, internal links to related lines, an FAQ, and a prominent quote button and phone number.

5. Turn reviews into a ranking engine

Reviews influence local rankings and whether people click. What matters is the number of reviews, how recent they are, the rating, and whether you reply.

  • Ask at moments of delight: after binding, after a smooth claim, at renewal, after a premium saving.
  • Send a direct review link by text and email. Make it a one-tap ask.
  • Reply to every review within a few days, including the negative ones, calmly and without sharing policy details.
  • Never offer incentives for reviews, and don't filter who you ask. Both break Google's policies.

6. Create content that answers policyholder questions

Helpful content builds topical authority and catches people early, before they ask for a quote. Good topics for agencies include:

  • Coverage explainers: "Does homeowners insurance cover water damage?"
  • Local risk guides: "Hurricane season insurance checklist for [region] homeowners"
  • Cost questions: "How much does general liability insurance cost for contractors in [state]?"
  • Life events: "Insurance checklist for first-time homebuyers"
  • Comparisons: "Independent agent vs. captive agent: what's the difference?"

Write with real expertise, show the licensed author or reviewer, and keep articles up to date. Google's quality guidelines put extra weight on experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust (E-E-A-T) for financial topics like insurance.

7. Technical SEO checklist

  • Mobile-friendly design that loads fast. Check Core Web Vitals in Search Console.
  • HTTPS on every page.
  • An XML sitemap submitted to Google Search Console.
  • LocalBusiness / InsuranceAgency schema markup on location pages.
  • No duplicate content from old or acquired agency sites. Use 301 redirects when merging domains.
  • Quote forms that work on mobile and send to the right inbox or CRM.
  • Click-to-call phone numbers in the header.

8. Track leads, not just rankings

Rankings are a means to an end. Set up:

  • Google Analytics 4 with quote-form submissions as key events.
  • Call tracking with dynamic number insertion, since many insurance leads call.
  • UTM tags on your Google Business Profile website links, so map pack leads show up separately.
  • A lead source field in your AMS/CRM, so you can tie organic leads to bound premium.

9. Where Google Ads and reactivation fit

SEO compounds, but it takes months. Google Ads fills the gap with immediate visibility and gives you keyword data to guide SEO. And every lead that doesn't bind today, from SEO or ads, should go into a reactivation sequence timed to their renewal date. Together, the three channels give a larger agency a predictable pipeline it controls.
